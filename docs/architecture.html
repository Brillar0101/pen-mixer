How a pen ends up controlling a filter

Numbers on this page are measured on the working rig, not estimated. Where something is estimated or unverified it says so.

Signal chain

Two transports, one discard path, and two loops. The loops are the parts that took longest to get right, so they are drawn rather than described.

The 197 Hz gap: USB scheduling plus CircuitPython's print() overhead against a 250 Hz target. It has not caused problems, but it means the smoothing figures below settle slightly slower in practice than the table says.

Production board

13 x 35 mm, four layers, 0.8 mm, all SMD. Two constraints drive the whole layout, and one of them dictates the board's shape.

Unverified: the ISP1807 land pattern in stylus.pretty/ was reconstructed from the datasheet drawing. Pad numbering is read from the datasheet figure and is correct; the coordinates are derived. On a 0.65 mm pitch 78-pad LGA, check it against Insight SiP's official land pattern before paying for fabrication.

Smoothing

Measured settling to 95% of a step at 250 Hz. Past roughly 20 ms end to end the instrument stops feeling connected to your hand.

Smoothing eats 24 of the 32 ms budget and it is the only term you can change freely. A one-pole filter picks one setting for both fast and slow motion. A one-euro filter adapts instead, and that is the right fix once the mapping stops changing.
